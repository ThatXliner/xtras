# Critic lenses by medium

Give the two critics different lenses so they cover different ground. For each medium, pick the pair below; when a piece mixes media (a video with narration and on-screen copy), pick the two lenses where it's most likely to fail, and fold the truth check into one of them.

Every critic, whatever its lens, also checks the work against taste.md and the references.

## Video, animation, trailers

- **Animation and acting:** how often characters change pose or expression compared with the reference; whether each line's verb is acted out; reactions to story beats; props and backgrounds that move; impact and weight on hits. One line per shot on what should happen instead.
- **Camera, editing and pacing:** cuts per 10 s; shot length; camera movement within shots; repeated framings; whether the opening hooks within 2 s; dead stretches; text over faces; anything popping in from off-frame. The 3–5 worst stretches by timestamp and how to recut them.
- **(Alternative) Sound and copy:** music fit and variety against the project's recent pieces, music level under speech using the loudest windows, sound effects that land on beats, plus every claim and the call to action checked against the facts file.

## Still images, illustration, art

- **Craft and style:** whether it matches the named style or reference; detail where the eye lands; anatomy, perspective and lighting errors; whether every object reads instantly as what it is; consistency with other pieces in the set.
- **Composition and message:** focal point, hierarchy, crop, legibility at the size it'll be seen (a phone feed, a thumbnail); whether the image says the one thing it's for.

## UI visuals and motion

- **Visual design:** fidelity to the brand and the existing design language, spacing and alignment, contrast and legibility, states (empty, loading, error), how it looks at phone width.
- **Motion and feel:** whether motion has a purpose, timing and easing, interruptibility, anything that feels sluggish, floaty or decorative; reduced-motion behaviour.

## Sound and music

- **Mix and fit:** loudness against the reference, speech intelligibility, genre fit for the brief, transitions, anything harsh or muddy in the spectrogram.
- **Variety and placement:** repeats against recent pieces, whether cues land on visual beats, silence used on purpose, whether effects add or clutter.

## Copy and marketing

- **Truth and specificity:** every claim checked against the facts file; numbers and availability exact; nothing a reader could call out as false or vague.
- **Voice and hook:** whether the first line earns the second, cheesy or stock phrasing, length against what the format can hold, and a clear call to action.
