# No Default UI

**Look:** a page that could only belong to this product. Every choice is deliberate; nothing is there because it's what an AI or a template does by default.
**Good for:** landing pages, marketing sites, app screens, UI mockups.

## The test

- **Could this exact page belong to a different product?** If yes, it fails. The direction comes from the product's audience, industry and materials, not from defaults.

## Defaults that read as AI

- **Purple or indigo-to-pink gradients**, violet as the primary colour, gradient washes.
- **Unconsidered type:** Inter, Roboto or system fonts chosen by default rather than on purpose.
- **The template layout:** three identical feature cards with an icon in a circle, every section in a card, everything centred, the same radius on everything.
- **Template tics:** one accented word in the headline, small all-caps labels above headings, 01/02/03 markers on things that aren't steps.
- **Motion everywhere:** fade-and-slide-up on every section, scale-on-hover on every card.
- **Emoji as icons**, placeholder images, lorem ipsum.

## Never fake it

- **No invented proof.** Fake statistics, testimonials, logo walls or "trusted by 10k+" are a blocker, not a smell.

## What good looks like

- **A deliberate palette** of 4–6 named colours, and one or two clearly different type families with a real scale.
- **Hierarchy over uniformity:** radius, shadow and size vary with importance; composition varies between sections.
- **One bold idea.** Spend the boldness on one memorable element and cut decoration that doesn't serve the brief.
- **One orchestrated motion moment** (for example the page load), plus motion that responds to the user. Respect reduced motion.
- **Plain, specific copy:** buttons say what they do ("Save changes", not "Submit" or "Get started").
- **A quality floor:** responsive at phone width, visible focus, accessible contrast (4.5:1 for body text).
