# Saturday Morning

**Look:** classic character animation: characters who think before they move, move with weight, and act out every line.
**Good for:** cartoons, animated explainers with characters, game cutscenes, mascots.
**Source:** the 12 principles of animation from Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation* (1981).

## Acting

- **Act out the line.** When the narration says a verb, a character does it. Standing and talking over a held pose is a fail.
- **Anticipation before action.** A wind-up before a jump, a glance before a turn, so the viewer knows what's coming.
- **Exaggerate to read.** Push poses and expressions further than real life so they read at a glance on a small screen.
- **Appeal.** Characters should be clear and pleasing to look at, with simple, readable shapes.

## Weight and motion

- **Squash and stretch** gives mass and flexibility; keep volume constant.
- **Follow-through and overlapping action:** hair, clothes and limbs keep moving after the body stops, and parts don't all move at once.
- **Arcs, not straight lines,** for natural movement.
- **Slow in and slow out** around key poses, with timing (the number of frames) that sells weight and mood.
- **Secondary action** supports the main one without competing with it.

## Staging

- **One clear idea per shot.** The pose, camera and composition make the main action unmistakable; nothing important happens off-frame.
- **Solid drawing:** characters keep their volume and proportions from every angle.
