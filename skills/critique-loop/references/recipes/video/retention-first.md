# Retention First

**Look:** the thumbnail's promise, delivered fast and kept all the way through. Built for YouTube, not the festival circuit.
**Good for:** YouTube videos, Shorts and Reels, anything judged by click-through and watch time.

## Packaging

- **Title and thumbnail come first, and the video must match them.** The package sets the viewer's expectation; a video that doesn't deliver it loses them, and watch time with them.
- **Judge the package as hard as the video.** It decides whether anyone sees the rest. A weak thumbnail sinks a good video.

## The first minute

- **Show the thumbnail's promise immediately.** Viewers who clicked for it leave if they have to wait. A slow intro, a logo sting or a "before we start" is a fail.
- **Put a wow moment early.** Something viewers didn't expect to see, in the first 30 seconds, that tells them this is worth staying for.

## Holding attention

- **Every stretch has a job.** The opening, the middle and the end each do something different to keep people watching; a section that only repeats what came before is a drop-off point.
- **Protect what the video depends on.** The few elements it can't work without get checked twice, never left to chance.
- **Spend where it shows on camera.** Polish nobody can see on screen doesn't count; neither does "Hollywood" polish that slows the video down.

## Measuring

- **Click-through, average view duration and average view percentage are the scorecard.** When critics argue about a cut, ask which choice keeps more people watching.
